# QuickRN Question Bank Blueprint
Version: 1.1
Purpose: Source taxonomy for the 600-question published bank: 500 core topic-bank items plus 100 NGN-format extension items.

## 1. Generation policy
- Treat this document as a topic blueprint, not as an answer key or proof that a recalled answer is correct.
- Generate original, clinically accurate questions; do not reproduce proprietary NCLEX items.
- Validate clinical claims, answer keys, rationales, medication details, and current practice against authoritative nursing/clinical references before publication.
- Do not infer missing answer choices or missing case details from examinee recollections.
- Preserve the intended learning objective when normalizing spelling or terminology.
- Avoid duplicate questions. A topic may produce multiple distinct questions only when they assess different objectives, patient populations, settings, or clinical-judgment steps.
- Balance the 500-item core bank across the categories below. The published runtime bank also includes 100 NGN extension items (600 total); do not describe the core count as the total published count. Use the topic list as a coverage map, not a requirement to give every bullet equal weight.
- Each item must include: specialty/category, topic tags, client need/clinical concept, cognitive level, format, stem, options or response structure, keyed answer, rationale for every option where applicable, and source/reference metadata.
- Use safe, unambiguous wording, realistic vital signs/labs, age/context where clinically relevant, and one defensible best answer unless the item is explicitly select-all-that-apply or another NGN format.

## 2. Specialty and topic taxonomy

### A. Maternal-newborn / obstetric nursing
- Placental abruption — NGN; maternal assessment, fetal status, hemorrhage, immediate priorities.
- Umbilical cord prolapse/protrusion — emergency response, relieving cord compression, fetal monitoring.
- Fetal heart-rate interpretation — VEAL CHOP mnemonic as a memory aid; distinguish early, variable, and late decelerations and select appropriate interventions.
- Chorionic villus sampling — indications, timing, preparation, risks, post-procedure teaching.
- Premature rupture of membranes (PROM) — infection surveillance, fetal assessment, gestational-age considerations, management.
- Preeclampsia and severe features — maternal/fetal assessment, seizure precautions, escalation.
- Magnesium sulfate for preeclampsia — therapeutic purpose, monitoring, toxicity, antidote, nursing actions.
- HELLP syndrome — recognition, laboratory findings, maternal/fetal risks, urgent management.
- Labor stages and latent phase — cervical dilation, contraction pattern, assessment and support.
- Macrosomia and shoulder dystocia — risk recognition and emergency team response.
- Newborn care: thermoregulation and assessment during the first 12 hours after birth.
- Teaching parents of a 4-day-old newborn what stool/urine findings to report.
- Obstetric case studies integrating maternal assessment, fetal monitoring, PROM, preeclampsia, and complications.

### B. Cardiovascular / perfusion
- Atrial fibrillation — ECG recognition, rate/rhythm implications, stroke risk, anticoagulation safety.
- Sinus bradycardia — ECG recognition, symptoms, assessment, escalation.
- ECG interpretation fundamentals and rhythm discrimination.
- Cardiac tamponade — NGN; assessment cues, hemodynamic compromise, priority response.
- Congestive heart failure (CHF) — fluid status, symptoms, medications, diet, worsening condition.
- Myocardial infarction — suspected MI with initially negative troponin; serial testing and ongoing assessment.
- Prinzmetal (variant) angina — coronary vasospasm, presentation, management principles.
- Implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) — purpose, discharge teaching, device precautions.
- Hypertension and severe blood pressure elevation in acute neurologic presentations.
- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) — prevention, recognition, interventions, anticoagulation safety.
- Pulmonary embolism (PE) — recognition, urgent priorities, DVT/PE case study.
- Peripheral arterial disease (PAD) — ischemia, dependent rubor, positioning, skin/foot care, exercise and teaching.
- Peripheral perfusion assessment and comparison of arterial versus venous disease.

### C. Respiratory / airway
- COPD — assessment, oxygenation, exacerbation management, inhaler teaching; case study.
- Asthma — peak-flow meter purpose and zones, triggers, rescue/controller medications.
- Metered-dose inhaler (MDI) — correct technique, spacer use, sequence and teaching.
- Pulse oximetry — interpretation, limitations, nursing response to abnormal readings.
- Total laryngectomy — airway care, communication, emergency airway considerations, PPE and secretion management.
- Airway priorities during seizure onset and other acute deterioration.
- CHF versus pneumonia — differential assessment cues and prioritization.

### D. Endocrine / metabolic
- Addison disease — manifestations, adrenal crisis, medication and sick-day teaching.
- Cushing syndrome — manifestations, complications, infection risk, nursing care.
- Hyperthyroidism — assessment, complications, treatment and teaching; case study.
- Hypothyroidism — manifestations and safety considerations; distinguish expected from concerning findings.
- Thyroidectomy — post-operative airway, bleeding, hypocalcemia and other complications.
- Diabetes mellitus (DM) — case study, glucose monitoring, medication, nutrition and complication prevention.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — initial fluid resuscitation, potassium assessment, insulin sequencing and monitoring.
- Arterial blood gas (ABG) interpretation, including respiratory alkalosis and compensation.

### E. Renal / urinary
- Hemodialysis — expected assessment findings after initial treatments, fluid removal, hypotension and disequilibrium concerns.
- Hemodialysis nutrition — sodium, potassium, phosphorus, fluid and protein considerations individualized to the plan of care.
- Urinary tract infection (UTI) — symptoms, prevention, older-adult assessment and treatment teaching.
- Post-TURP care as a delegation/assignment and prioritization context.
- Dialysis access assessment and protection.

### F. Neurologic
- Stroke — NGN; ischemic versus hemorrhagic cues, time-sensitive assessment, dysphagia, safety, rehabilitation and secondary prevention.
- Stroke recognition: unilateral weakness, facial droop, aphasia and severe hypertension; distinguish from mimics such as Bell palsy.
- Severe headache after lumbar puncture — assessment, post-procedure complication recognition and escalation.
- Seizures — protect from injury, maintain airway, timing, positioning, postictal care; do not restrain or place objects in the mouth.
- Autonomic dysreflexia — triggers, immediate positioning and urgent blood-pressure management.
- Alzheimer disease — cognition, safety, communication, caregiver support and progression.
- Cerebral palsy — function-focused care, mobility and appropriate assistive devices.
- Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) — ascending weakness, respiratory monitoring and differentiation from meningitis or other neurologic disorders.
- Meningitis — precautions, assessment, urgent treatment and differentiation from GBS.
- Aphasia and communication support.

### G. Musculoskeletal / mobility
- Buck's traction — setup, alignment, weights, skin/neurovascular assessment and complications.
- Fractures and long-bone fractures — neurovascular checks, fat embolism risk, immobilization and case study.
- Compartment syndrome — severe pain, especially pain with passive stretch; urgent escalation and limb-threatening ischemia. Do not delay escalation by merely giving PRN analgesia or independently adjusting a cast.
- Total hip replacement — positioning, movement precautions according to surgical approach, mobility and DVT prevention.
- Osteoporosis — risk factors, prevention, falls and medication teaching.
- Sickle cell anemia — vaso-occlusive crisis, pain, hydration, oxygenation when indicated, infection prevention and complications.
- Ventrogluteal injection — landmarking, safe technique and rationale.

### H. Hematology / immune / oncology-related
- Hemophilia — bleeding precautions, factor replacement principles, injury prevention.
- Iron deficiency and iron absorption — administration and food interactions; distinguish evidence-based teaching from myths.
- Sickle cell anemia (also cross-tagged under musculoskeletal/hematology).
- Latex allergy — cross-reactive foods including banana; assess individual allergy history and avoid overgeneralizing cross-reactivity.
- Mantoux/tuberculin skin test — interpretation by induration and risk category, not redness alone; isolation decisions must be based on suspected/confirmed infectious pulmonary or laryngeal TB and facility policy, not a skin test result alone.
- Acid-fast bacilli (AFB) testing — interpret in clinical context; distinguish screening tests from evidence of active contagious disease.

### I. Infectious disease / isolation / precautions
- Diphtheria — transmission-based precautions, airway risk, antitoxin/antibiotic principles and public-health response.
- Pertussis — droplet precautions, treatment and prevention.
- Clostridioides difficile (C. difficile) — contact/enteric precautions, hand hygiene with soap and water, environmental cleaning.
- Botulism — foodborne/wound/infant forms, recognition, respiratory monitoring and precautions appropriate to transmission route.
- Measles (rubeola) — airborne precautions, immunity and exposure management.
- Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) — contact/droplet practices per current facility guidance, supportive care and pediatric risk.
- Herpes zoster — localized versus disseminated disease and appropriate precautions based on immune status and extent.
- Impetigo — contact precautions, hygiene and treatment teaching.
- Meningitis — organism-dependent isolation precautions.
- Alcohol withdrawal and delirium tremens — cross-tagged under behavioral health and emergency care.

### J. Gastrointestinal / hepatic
- Liver cirrhosis — ascites, bleeding, encephalopathy, nutrition and medication safety.
- Asterixis (liver flap) — recognition as a possible sign of hepatic encephalopathy; assess mental status and contributing factors.
- Ulcerative colitis — manifestations, complications, medication and nutrition; distinguish from other GI conditions.
- Colonoscopy preparation and discharge teaching — bowel cleansing, sedation/anesthesia expectations, transportation, and what intake restrictions apply to the prescribed preparation.
- Gastric tube feeding — verify placement per policy, aspiration risk and response to unexpected residuals; do not assume a universal residual-volume threshold.
- Iron absorption and nutrition teaching (cross-tagged with hematology).

### K. Behavioral health / mental health / substance use
- Alcohol withdrawal — case study, symptom progression, seizure risk, CIWA-Ar when appropriate, medication and safety.
- Delirium tremens — recognition as a severe, potentially life-threatening withdrawal complication; distinguish from uncomplicated withdrawal.
- Benzodiazepines — safety, sedation, dependence, overdose risk and suicide precautions where clinically indicated.
- Chlordiazepoxide — role in alcohol withdrawal, monitoring and precautions.
- Antipsychotic/psychotropic medication safety — identify findings or orders that require clarification; specify the exact drug and clinical context.
- Aripiprazole — indication, adverse effects, monitoring and patient teaching.
- Fluoxetine — SSRI effects, onset, adverse effects and safety.
- Sertraline — SSRI teaching, sexual adverse effects, adherence and discontinuation precautions.
- Delusions — therapeutic communication, safety and reality-based responses without reinforcing the delusion.
- Elder abuse — immediate safety, private assessment, trust-building, documentation and mandatory reporting requirements.
- Autism spectrum disorder — communication, sensory needs and individualized support; avoid reducing assessment to a single sign such as eye contact.

### L. Pharmacology / medication teaching
- Digoxin — apical pulse, toxicity cues, interactions, monitoring and patient teaching.
- Statins — lipid-lowering purpose, muscle symptoms, liver-related precautions and adherence.
- Alendronate — administration with plain water on an empty stomach; remain upright for the required period; follow product-specific instructions.
- Ticlopidine — antiplatelet action, serious blood dyscrasias and monitoring; distinguish from anticoagulants.
- Riluzole — administration timing in relation to meals and product formulation; liver monitoring and teaching.
- Carisoprodol — skeletal muscle relaxant, CNS depression, dependence/misuse risk and safety.
- Carvedilol — nonselective beta-blocker with alpha-1 blocking activity; indications, pulse/BP monitoring and precautions.
- Indomethacin — NSAID use in gout/inflammation; GI, renal and cardiovascular risks.
- Feverfew — potential interaction with warfarin/bleeding risk; assess supplement use and advise clinician consultation.
- Magnesium sulfate — cross-tagged under obstetric nursing; toxicity monitoring and antidote.
- Medication reconciliation, adverse effects, contraindications, interactions and patient education across all listed drugs.

### M. Pediatrics / growth and development
- Developmental milestones — 15-month-old child and block-stacking expectations; use age-appropriate, evidence-based milestone ranges.
- School-age child — obesity prevention, nutrition, activity and family-centered teaching.
- Autism spectrum disorder — developmental assessment and individualized communication.
- Premature infant/newborn feeding — aspiration risk, feeding assessment and safe response to unexpected findings.
- Newborn thermoregulation and early post-delivery priorities.
- Infant stool and urine output teaching at 4 days of age.
- RSV, pertussis, and other pediatric infection precautions.

### N. Fundamentals / safety / ethics / professional practice
- Prioritization — ABCs, acute versus chronic, unstable versus stable, unexpected versus expected, safety and time-sensitive threats.
- Delegation to UAP — tasks appropriate to role, stable predictable patients, reporting and RN accountability.
- Delegation to LPN/LVN — routine care for stable patients within scope and policy; assessment, initial teaching and evaluation remain RN responsibilities as applicable.
- RN/LPN/UAP scope — apply jurisdiction-specific rules and facility policy; do not assume identical scope across locations.
- Assignment of a float nurse — match demonstrated competency, orientation, patient stability and supervision; do not assign solely by convenience.
- OB nurse floated to the emergency department — assignment must reflect competency and local policy; do not assume specialty nurses can independently manage unfamiliar emergency patients.
- UAP transfer from bed to wheelchair — assess stability and mobility needs first; delegate only when safe and within role.
- Private-room allocation and isolation — prioritize transmission risk and organism-specific precautions; a positive Mantoux test alone does not establish contagious TB.
- Do-not-intubate (DNI) order and family disagreement — clarify documented patient wishes/orders, assess decision-making/surrogate authority, communicate urgently with provider/team, and follow law and policy; do not override a valid directive solely at family request.
- Veracity — truthful communication and professional ethics.
- End-of-life care, advance directives, goals of care and family communication.
- Therapeutic communication and establishing trust and rapport.
- Quantitative care / quality improvement — interpret measures, outcomes and improvement data.
- Staff recognition and motivation — distinguish recognition, appreciation, incentives and performance feedback.
- Colonoscopy consent, preparation, sedation safety and discharge transportation.
- PPE selection and standard/transmission-based precautions.
- Latex allergy and environmental safety.
- Fall prevention, safe patient handling and assistive devices.

## 3. Integrated case-study blueprints
Build multi-question unfolding cases with a consistent patient record, evolving cues and clinically meaningful changes. Include trend data and ask the learner to recognize cues, analyze cues, prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions, take action and evaluate outcomes.

1. Atrial fibrillation with acute stroke: rhythm, onset/time last known well, neurologic findings, dysphagia, blood pressure and safety.
2. PROM: gestational age, maternal temperature, fluid characteristics, fetal heart rate and infection risk.
3. Diabetes mellitus / DKA: glucose, ketones, ABGs, electrolytes, hydration, fluids and insulin.
4. Long-bone fracture after fall: pain, neurovascular status, compartment syndrome, fat embolism and mobility.
5. DVT progressing to suspected PE: unilateral leg findings, respiratory symptoms, oxygenation and emergency response.
6. COPD exacerbation: baseline status, work of breathing, oxygenation, ABG and inhaler/medication use.
7. Hyperthyroidism: vital signs, thyroid storm cues, treatment and monitoring.
8. Alcohol withdrawal progressing toward delirium tremens: time since last drink, autonomic findings, mental status, seizure risk and medication.
9. PAD: limb symptoms, skin findings, pulses, positioning, wound care and education.
10. Macrosomia with shoulder dystocia: risk factors, recognition, team response and maternal/newborn assessment.
11. CHF versus pneumonia: overlapping dyspnea, lung sounds, imaging/labs, fluid status and response to interventions.
12. Cirrhosis with hepatic encephalopathy: asterixis, mental status, precipitating factors, bleeding risk and nutrition.
13. Ulcerative colitis: stool pattern, dehydration, bleeding, pain, complications and treatment.
14. Meningitis versus GBS: distinct assessment patterns, airway/respiratory risk, precautions and urgent interventions.
15. Preeclampsia with severe features: blood pressure, headache/visual symptoms, DTRs, clonus, edema, labs, magnesium sulfate and fetal status.
16. Mixed six-patient prioritization set: six unrelated patients with competing acuity, abnormal cues, isolation needs and delegation opportunities. Each patient must have enough information to support a defensible ranking.

## 4. Priority and delegation question patterns
Use these as recurring item archetypes:
- Severe pain with passive stretch in a casted/fractured limb: suspect compartment syndrome and escalate immediately; do not make PRN analgesia the sole response.
- Severe headache after lumbar puncture: assess and identify post-procedure complications; use the specific clinical context rather than reflexively applying outdated flat-bed-rest rules.
- Seizure onset: protect from injury, position to maintain airway when possible, time the seizure, and summon help; do not restrain.
- Late fetal heart-rate decelerations: recognize uteroplacental insufficiency and initiate appropriate intrauterine resuscitation/escalation per current protocol.
- Cord prolapse: relieve pressure on the cord and activate emergency obstetric response.
- Suspected stroke: immediate focused assessment and time-sensitive stroke pathway.
- DNI order conflict: respect valid patient directive and clarify with the responsible clinician/surrogate under applicable law and policy.
- One private room among patients with positive/unknown TB testing: use active infectiousness, symptoms, diagnostic evidence and airborne isolation requirements; a TST result alone is not sufficient.
- Float nurse assignment: competency-based assignment and appropriate supervision.
- LPN/LVN and UAP delegation: scope, predictability, stability, task complexity and RN accountability.
- Six-patient prioritization: compare airway/breathing/circulation, acute neurologic change, bleeding, sepsis, unstable vitals and time-sensitive treatment.

## 5. NGN item design
Use NGN formats where they genuinely fit the objective:
- Unfolding case study with six clinical-judgment steps.
- Matrix/grid for comparing findings or interventions.
- Multiple response / select all that apply.
- Extended multiple response.
- Cloze/drop-down for interpretation or completion.
- Highlight text for salient cues.
- Trend item using serial vitals, labs or assessment findings.
- Bow-tie: condition, actions and parameters to monitor.
- Prioritization/ranking when a defensible sequence exists.

Do not label an ordinary single-best-answer item as NGN merely because it uses a clinical scenario. Keep all case-study data internally consistent.

## 7. Quality and safety checks before publication
- Verify each answer against current authoritative references and local scope-of-practice context where relevant.
- Do not treat candidate-recalled selected answers as correct.
- Check for unsafe absolutes, outdated mnemonics presented as clinical rules, and unsupported food-drug claims.
- Clarify that Mantoux/TST indicates immune sensitization and is interpreted by induration/risk; it does not alone diagnose active contagious TB.
- Verify current isolation guidance for measles, pertussis, RSV, zoster, C. difficile, meningitis and TB.
- Verify drug-specific administration instructions, especially formulations and meal timing.
- Do not use a universal gastric residual cutoff without specifying policy and evidence.
- Do not make a negative initial troponin rule out MI; include timing and serial evaluation where appropriate.
- Do not make a single developmental milestone, such as eye contact or a specific block count, diagnostic by itself.
- Distinguish DNI from DNR and explain that treatment goals must follow the actual documented directive and applicable law.
- Keep answer options parallel, plausible and mutually distinguishable; avoid cueing the answer by length or grammar.
- Ensure rationales explain why the correct action is safest and why distractors are less appropriate.
- Track references, review status, version and publication status for every item.
